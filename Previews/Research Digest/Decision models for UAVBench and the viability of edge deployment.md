# Decision models for UAVBench and the viability of edge deployment

**Source:** [ArXiv – cs.AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28331)
**Imported:** 2026-11-24 15:22 UTC

## Abstract / Summary

arXiv:2611.28331v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Small language models are often proposed for reasoning onboard a drone. However, a good benchmark score does not tell us whether a model will actually run, and run usefully, on an edge computer. In this paper we study three related questions using the UAV decision benchmark, UAVBench. First, are small structured decision models good candidates for these tasks? Second, can an ordinary local machine (a Mac) screen candidate models before we test them on physical devices? Third, what software and resource limits govern deployment? Our main Mac evaluation covers 24 model configurations on the same 2,000 validated multiple-choice questions from UAVBench. These include 20 size-and-precision configurations of 11 generative models, plus four open-weight structured decision models that received no additional UAVBench-specific training. A structured decision model, H2O Lightning 4B, achieved the highest accuracy we observed, scoring 77.65% on the Mac. This shows that these models are well suited to this kind of task. However, when we tried to deploy the decision models, we ran into conversion and runtime limits on the edge devices we tested, which we describe in detail. To test local screening, we ran matched evaluations of two generative models on both the Mac and a Qualcomm RB3 board. The accuracies did not match exactly, but the ranking did. LFM2 2.6B ranked above Qwen2.5 1.5B on both platforms. This supports using a local machine to shortlist candidates. That said, individual answers still differed between platforms, so testing on the actual hardware remained necessary.
