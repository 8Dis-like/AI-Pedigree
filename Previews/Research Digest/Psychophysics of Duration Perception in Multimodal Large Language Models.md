# Psychophysics of Duration Perception in Multimodal Large Language Models

**Source:** [ArXiv – cs.CL (Computation & Language / NLP)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33176)
**Imported:** 2026-11-26 05:54 UTC

## Abstract / Summary

arXiv:2611.33176v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Studies of time perception in language models focus mainly on text-based settings. Yet multimodal models are increasingly used for tasks that require perceiving the passage of time, making the limits of their temporal perception important to understand. We measure the limits of duration perception from audio against a human baseline to address this gap. Drawing from psychophysics, we operationalise duration perception as a melody recognition task, testing when a melody becomes unrecognisable as it is slowed down. Of 21 tested models, most cannot recognise well-known melodies without labelled in-context examples. With in-context examples, seven recognise melodies reliably, most peaking within the median human recognition band. Generalisation is limited: (i) recognition peaks shift with in-context example speed, (ii) melody titles induce prediction bias, and (iii) providing examples with scientific pitch notation does not prevent accuracy declining at long note durations for most models. In humans, the recognition window is a property of the perceiver; in current models, it is largely a property of the prompt.
