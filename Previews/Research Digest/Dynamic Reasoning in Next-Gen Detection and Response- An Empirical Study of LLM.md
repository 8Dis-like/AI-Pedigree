# Dynamic Reasoning in Next-Gen Detection and Response: An Empirical Study of LLM Triage, Reinforcement Tuning, and Adversarial Evasion

## Abstract / Summary

arXiv:2611.33470v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We present an end-to-end empirical study of dynamic reasoning in next-generation detection and response pipelines, evaluating how static classifiers, graph-based signal fusion, and large language model (LLM) triage agents perform under strict human-analyst attention budgets (Recall at B alerts/day). Across 56 reproducible experiments spanning a multi-layer enterprise-and-cloud simulator and four public datasets (SOREL-20M, BODMAS, LANL, and DARPA Transparent Computing), we test twelve architectural claims governing modern fleet-scale defense. First, we show that fleet growth halves recall at a fixed analyst budget even at negligible compute cost (<$0.0003 per million events), and that rarity-weighted graph risk propagation outperforms GraphSAGE without neural training. Second, evaluating frontier reasoning models (Gemini) and fine-tuned open-weight models (Qwen3-4B) for alert triage reveals that unconstrained LLM auto-closure eliminates 90% of false positives while silently discarding 31-44% of true attack cases. Third, post-training with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) causes severe reward hacking on unseen attack classes, suppressing up to 86% of novel attacks compared to two-epoch supervised fine-tuning. Finally, against an adaptive adversary generating plausible cover stories in benign telemetry fields, LLM triage evasion rises sharply unless gated by a deterministic policy harness prior to model invocation.
