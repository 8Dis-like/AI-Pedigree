# Scaling Laws of Ensemble Uncertainty in Language Models

**Source:** [ArXiv – cs.NE (Neural & Evolutionary Computing)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32801)
**Imported:** 2026-11-25 06:00 UTC

## Abstract / Summary

arXiv:2611.32801v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Many use cases for large language models (LLMs)---from AI safety to exploration and human interaction---can benefit from epistemic uncertainty estimates. We argue that existing Bayesian Deep Learning solutions, which are often restricted to a fixed model family, fail to capture the full epistemic uncertainty at practical LLM scales. This is because the error due to the bounded capacity of the model family remains invisible to disagreement among models supported by the prior. We relate this error to the well-known power-law scaling of LLM loss with model size. We then show that a second power law quantifies the shrinking disagreement within ensembles as model size increases. Its slower decay relative to the capacity law above suggests that ensembles capture a growing share of epistemic uncertainty and become more competitive as an axis for scaling, compared to a single larger model. Finally, a third power law quantifies the loss of ensemble diversity as members share a larger fraction of training before branching, and therefore identifies a further limitation, beyond the capacity error, for LoRA ensembles and optimization-based or second-order methods that approximate uncertainty around a single training solution. At a high level, these findings suggest diverse ensembles can be a promising axis, both for scaling and uncertainty quantification at larger scales.
