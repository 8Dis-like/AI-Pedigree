# Chain-of-Thought Is Not Explainability

**Source:** [ArXiv – cs.LG (Machine Learning)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31271)
**Imported:** 2026-11-25 06:00 UTC

## Abstract / Summary

arXiv:2611.31271v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Chain-of-thought (CoT) allows language models to verbalise multi-step rationales before producing their final answer. While this technique often boosts task performance and offers an impression of transparency into the model's reasoning, this position paper argues that rationales generated by current CoT techniques can be misleading and are neither necessary nor sufficient for trustworthy interpretability. We analyse faithfulness as whether CoTs are not only human-interpretable but also reflect the model's underlying reasoning well enough to support responsible use. By synthesising evidence from prior work, we systematically show that verbalised chains are frequently unfaithful, diverging from the critical hidden computations that drive a model's predictions. As CoT is increasingly relied upon in application domains, including high-stakes ones such as medicine, law, and autonomous systems, we further argue that this unfaithfulness is not taken seriously enough in these settings: our analysis of 1,000 recent CoT-centric papers finds that approximately 21.7% explicitly treat CoT as a load-bearing interpretability technique, building pipelines or safety arguments that would not hold if CoT is unfaithful to the model's actual reasoning. They include papers in high-stakes domains that heavily hinge on such interpretability claims. Building on prior work, we make three proposals: (i) avoid treating CoT as being sufficient for interpretability without additional verification, (ii) adopt rigorous methods that assess faithfulness for downstream decision-making, and (iii) report such assessments alongside any interpretability claims based on CoT, while continuing to use CoT for its communicative and monitoring benefits.
