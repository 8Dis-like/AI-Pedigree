# Learning What vs. Learning How: Decomposing Task and Interface Competence in Agentic Training

**Source:** [ArXiv – cs.AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31427)
**Imported:** 2026-11-25 06:00 UTC

## Abstract / Summary

arXiv:2611.31427v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: LLM agents operate through harnesses that define system prompts, action formats, feedback loops, and resource constraints. A model may possess the knowledge needed to solve a task yet fail to express that knowledge through a particular harness. This raises a basic question: when a policy fails under a given harness, is it missing task knowledge or the competence to operate the interface? We distinguish two components of agentic competence: task competence, the knowledge and reasoning needed to solve a task, and interface competence, the ability to act on that knowledge through a given harness. Across four reasoning and agentic benchmarks, we show that harness-conditioned training yields vastly different outcomes depending on how these interact. Multi-harness distillation aggregates task knowledge from trajectories collected through different harnesses into a shared policy, but this knowledge is realised only when training preserves or improves competence on the inference-time interface. Otherwise, single-harness training can induce interference in interface competence, sometimes catastrophically collapsing performance on a held-out harness despite improvements in underlying task knowledge. To disentangle task and interface supervision, we introduce harness rehearsal: distillation on traces that carry minimal task knowledge while exercising the target interface. Cross-harness task supervision or rehearsal alone yields little gain, but their combination yields gains far larger than either: on BFCL V3 multi-turn with Qwen3-4B, they achieve 8.5% and 32.5% respectively, versus 58.8% together. Finally, on-policy reinforcement learning (RL) is more robust than distillation to single-harness training: it does not substantially degrade performance on other harnesses, and interface competence acquired through multi-harness distillation persists through subsequent RL.
