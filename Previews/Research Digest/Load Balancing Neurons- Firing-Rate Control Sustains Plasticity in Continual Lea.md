# Load Balancing Neurons: Firing-Rate Control Sustains Plasticity in Continual Learning

**Source:** [ArXiv – cs.NE (Neural & Evolutionary Computing)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32691)
**Imported:** 2026-11-25 06:00 UTC

## Abstract / Summary

arXiv:2611.32691v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Neural networks in continual learning often lose plasticity: some neurons become inactive, while others fire almost constantly. This limits adaptation to shifting data and wastes capacity. Prior work mitigates this by periodically reinitializing low-utility units, but such resets can destroy previously learned features and do not proactively prevent low utility. We study a simple diagnostic measure: the firing rate of ReLU units, defined as the fraction of positive pre-activations. Low rates identify dead units, while very high rates indicate linearized, always-on units. Based on this view, we introduce a lightweight load-balancing mechanism that adjusts per-neuron thresholds to keep firing rates within a target range. Across continual ImageNet and class-incremental CIFAR-100, firing-rate distributions explain differences in plasticity across a range of approaches, including well-known techniques such as L2 regularization and non-affine normalization, and load balancing matches or exceeds reset-based methods on both benchmarks. The same mechanism carries over to reinforcement learning, improving Rainbow DQN on Atari over a LayerNorm baseline.
